Module 01 · Risk Vectors

AI Safety & Misuse Risks

Threat Level
CRITICAL

AI tools can be weaponized — through voice cloning, deepfakes, AI-generated phishing, and increasingly sophisticated fraud. These aren't theoretical future risks. They are happening now, at scale, and the barrier to entry drops every month. Knowing what these attacks look like is your first line of defence.

🎣
AI Phishing Surge
+1,265%
Increase in phishing emails since ChatGPT launched (SlashNext, 2023)
🗣️
Voice Clone Threshold
3 sec
Amount of audio needed to clone a voice with modern AI tools
💸
AI Fraud Losses
$25B+
Estimated annual losses from AI-enabled financial fraud (Forbes, 2024)
🎭
Deepfake Growth
+550%
Increase in deepfake content online, 2019–2023 (Deeptrace Labs)
NCSC/GCHQ Assessment · January 2024

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre found that AI is already lowering the barrier to cyberattacks, particularly for reconnaissance and spear-phishing. The report concluded AI will "almost certainly" increase the volume and impact of cyberattacks over the next two years, with critical national infrastructure at highest risk.

↳ The near-term impact of AI on the cyber threat — NCSC/GCHQ, 2024
📞
Urgent calls from "family" asking for money — voice clone scams impersonating loved ones in fake emergencies
📧
Hyper-personalized phishing emails referencing real details about you — AI makes targeted attacks cheap and scalable
🎥
Video calls from apparent colleagues or executives behaving "off" — deepfake impersonation is increasingly convincing
🔒
AI-generated CSAM — synthetic content is illegal in most jurisdictions; report immediately to NCMEC CyberTipline
Voice clone scamsAI-generated phishingDeepfake identity fraudAI-assisted hackingWatermarking (emerging)Detection tools available
// Action Plan — AI Safety & Misuse
STEP 01
Create a family code word — use it to verify identity if someone calls claiming to be a loved one in an emergency. Voice clones can't know it.
STEP 02
Verify urgent requests independently — hang up, find the real number, call back. AI scams always manufacture urgency. Never act on financial pressure in the moment.
STEP 03
Scrutinize personalized emails — AI generates convincing targeted messages. Check sender addresses carefully; never click unexpected links.
STEP 04
Watermark your creative work — Adobe Content Credentials (C2PA standard) embeds provenance data into images and video you create.
STEP 05
Report AI-generated CSAM — in the U.S., submit to NCMEC's CyberTipline at missingkids.org. All AI-generated sexual content involving minors is criminal.

Module 02 · Information Integrity

Hallucinations & Misinformation

Threat Level
HIGH

AI language models can produce outputs that are fluent, authoritative, and completely fabricated. This is called "hallucination" — the model generates statistically plausible text with no grounding in actual fact. When AI-generated misinformation is also cheap to produce at scale, the implications for public discourse are serious.

🤥
Hallucination Rate
3–27%
Varies by model and task — highest for factual Q&A and citations
⚖️
Legal Consequences
Real
Multiple lawyers sanctioned for citing entirely fabricated AI-generated case law in court filings
🗞️
AI News Farms
100s
Apparently legitimate news websites found to be mostly AI-generated content (NewsGuard, 2023)
😐
Confident & Wrong
Worst case
Models are often most confident precisely when they are most factually wrong
Nature Machine Intelligence · Augenstein et al. · 2023

Researchers found that large language models fail to reliably distinguish true from false claims across a range of fact-checking benchmarks. Models' expressed confidence was poorly calibrated — they were often most confident when wrong. The researchers called this "confident hallucination" and warned it poses serious risks in medical, legal, and journalistic contexts.

↳ Factuality Challenges in the Era of Large Language Models — Nature MI, 2023
📚
AI-generated citations — papers, case laws, studies that don't exist, described with convincing precision
🖼️
Out-of-context images recycled as new events — always reverse image search viral photos before trusting them
📰
AI-written articles on legitimate-looking sites — no real editorial oversight, accountability, or fact-checking
🔢
Precise-sounding statistics from no real source — specificity creates false credibility in AI outputs
// Action Plan — Hallucinations & Misinformation
STEP 01
Never cite AI outputs directly in professional, academic, or legal work. Every factual claim needs independent verification from a primary source.
STEP 02
Use AI for structure, not facts — AI excels at organizing ideas and drafting prose. It is unreliable for specific statistics, data, and citations.
STEP 03
Use detection tools — Sensity and Microsoft VASA for deepfakes; GPTZero for AI-written text; reverse image search for photos.
STEP 04
Treat AI output as a first draft — not a finished answer. Research, verification, and judgment remain entirely your responsibility.
STEP 05
Slow down before sharing — AI misinformation is designed to seem credible. Apply lateral reading and fact-check sites (Snopes, PolitiFact) before amplifying anything.

Module 03 · Data Sovereignty

Privacy & AI Surveillance

Threat Level
HIGH

AI has supercharged the ability to collect, process, and draw inferences from personal data. Facial recognition, language model chatbots, and smart home devices all create data trails that can reveal deeply personal information — often without meaningful consent from the person being profiled.

📷
AI Surveillance Cameras
770M+
AI-enabled cameras deployed worldwide (IHS Markit)
🗣️
Voice Assistants
Always On
Wake-word detection requires continuous audio monitoring of your environment
🧠
Inference Capability
Extreme
AI infers health status, sexuality, and political views from seemingly innocuous behavioral data
📥
Chatbot Data Risk
Real
Inputs to AI tools may be stored, reviewed, and used to train future model versions
PNAS · Matz, Kosinski, Nave & Stillwell · 2017

AI models using only Facebook likes could infer personality traits with greater accuracy than friends, family, or even spouses. When combined with targeted persuasion techniques, this enabled psychological manipulation at scale — a capability since implicated in political influence operations across multiple countries.

↳ Psychological targeting as an effective and ethical tool — PNAS, 114(48), 2017
🤖
Chatbots with unclear retention policies — does your conversation get stored and used to train future models?
👁️
Facial recognition check-ins at events, schools, or stores — often deployed without explicit or informed consent
📄
AI tools requesting sensitive document uploads — medical, legal, financial — with vague or broad privacy terms
📱
Apps requesting microphone, camera, or contacts access that aren't necessary for their core functionality
// Action Plan — Privacy & Surveillance
STEP 01
Read the privacy policy before using any AI tool — specifically: is input stored? Does it train future models? Can you opt out?
STEP 02
Never input sensitive personal data — medical records, legal documents, passwords, SSNs — unless you've fully verified how it's handled.
STEP 03
Use training opt-outs — Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini offer ways to prevent conversations from being used for model training. Find and enable them.
STEP 04
Know your legal rights — GDPR (EU), CCPA (California), and similar laws give you rights to access, correct, and delete data held about you.
STEP 05
Cover cameras and mics when idle — especially near always-on smart home devices. Physical controls are more reliable than software settings.

Module 04 · Cognitive Sovereignty

Over-Reliance & Critical Thinking

Threat Level
MODERATE

AI platforms are designed to be as capable and fluid as possible — which makes it easy to let them do your thinking. Research is already documenting a real effect: when AI handles the cognitive work, humans stop building the skills, judgment, and independence that come from doing it themselves.

🧠
Automation Bias
Documented
People accept AI recommendations even when they're obviously incorrect — simply because a computer said it
✍️
Writing Skills
Declining
Students using AI writing tools show measurably reduced independent composition ability (multiple studies, 2023–24)
🏥
Medical Deference
Observed
Clinicians shown to defer to AI diagnostics even when clinical evidence suggests a different conclusion
🎨
Creative Convergence
Narrowing
Heavy AI use associated with homogeneous outputs — everything begins to look and sound alike
Harvard Business School Working Paper · Bastani et al. · 2024

Studying 758 students, researchers found that those using AI on challenging problems performed significantly worse on subsequent tests without AI compared to control groups. The study concluded that AI creates "cognitive offloading" — outsourcing the mental effort that actually builds lasting skill and deep understanding.

↳ Generative AI Can Harm Learning — HBS Working Paper, 2024
🤔
Feeling unable to start a task — an essay, email, or problem — without consulting AI first
✅
Accepting AI output without reading or critically evaluating it — just copying and submitting
💬
Asking AI to make decisions that are yours to make — relationship choices, health decisions, life direction
📉
Using AI as a replacement for learning rather than a supplement — AI summaries instead of source material
// Action Plan — Over-Reliance & Critical Thinking
STEP 01
Attempt things yourself first — try the task before turning to AI. Even a rough draft keeps you cognitively engaged with the material.
STEP 02
Use AI to challenge your thinking — ask it to critique your ideas and find flaws in your reasoning. This keeps you in the driver's seat.
STEP 03
Keep AI-free zones — certain decisions, creative works, and learning activities should remain fully yours. Protect them explicitly and consistently.
STEP 04
Practice independent skills regularly — write without AI sometimes, do mental math, read full texts. Cognition is genuinely use-it-or-lose-it.
STEP 05
Distrust AI confidence — when an AI sounds certain, that is a design choice, not a measure of accuracy. Always apply your own judgment.

Module 05 · Fairness Systems

Algorithmic Bias & Fairness

Threat Level
MODERATE

AI systems learn from human-generated data — and that data encodes documented historical bias. When those patterns are baked into algorithms, they replicate and amplify existing inequalities in hiring, lending, healthcare, and criminal justice. Recognizing and challenging biased AI decisions is an essential skill.

👁️
Facial Rec. Error Gap
34.7%
Error rate for dark-skinned women vs 0.8% for light-skinned men (Buolamwini & Gebru)
🏦
Lending Algorithms
~2×
More likely to deny mortgages to Black applicants vs white applicants with similar credit profiles
💼
Hiring AI Failure
Amazon
Scrapped its own AI hiring tool after it penalized CVs referencing women's organizations (2018)
🏥
Healthcare Bias
56%
Of Black patients missed by a widely used healthcare algorithm due to a biased proxy variable (Obermeyer et al.)
Science · Obermeyer et al. · 2019

A healthcare algorithm used on tens of millions of patients systematically underestimated illness in Black patients, directing less care toward them. The bias arose because cost was used as a proxy for health need — but Black patients historically received less care, making them appear healthier in the data than they actually were.

↳ Dissecting racial bias in an algorithm used to manage the health of populations — Science, 366, 2019
ACM FAccT · Buolamwini & Gebru · 2018

The landmark "Gender Shades" audit tested facial analysis tools from Microsoft, IBM, and Face++. Error rates differed by up to 34.4 percentage points between light-skinned males and dark-skinned females. The study directly prompted responses from major technology companies and became foundational in AI fairness research.

↳ Gender Shades: Intersectional Accuracy Disparities in Commercial Gender Classification — ACM FAccT, 2018
// Action Plan — Algorithmic Bias
STEP 01
Question AI decisions that affect you — if a loan, job application, or medical result is unexpected, ask whether AI was used and request human review.
STEP 02
Demand transparency — organizations using AI for consequential decisions should explain how they were reached. "The algorithm decided" is not acceptable.
STEP 03
Support algorithmic auditing — advocate for independent third-party audits in institutions that affect your community: courts, banks, hospitals, employers.
STEP 04
Reject false objectivity — an algorithm isn't neutral because it's mathematical. It reflects the values, priorities, and historical data of those who built it.
STEP 05
Support diverse AI development — bias is measurably reduced when AI teams include diverse perspectives. Fund, amplify, and advocate for this work.

Module 06 · Human Agency

Autonomy & Human Agency

Threat Level
SYSTEMIC

The deepest question about AI isn't about any single tool — it's about who remains in control. As AI systems become embedded in decisions about employment, credit, healthcare, and information access, the question of whether humans are directing AI or AI is directing humans becomes genuinely important to answer.

🗳️
Political Influence
Documented
AI recommendation algorithms shift political views and voting intention (Epstein & Robertson, 2015)
📉
Jobs at Risk
300M
Jobs potentially affected by generative AI automation globally (Goldman Sachs, 2023)
🤖
AI in Consequential Decisions
Expanding
AI now determines outcomes in criminal sentencing, insurance pricing, and loan approvals
🌐
Governance Gap
Wide
The EU AI Act (2024) is the world's first comprehensive AI law. Most countries still lack equivalent frameworks.
PNAS · Epstein & Robertson · 2015

A biased search ranking could shift voting preferences of undecided voters by 20% or more, with participants showing no awareness they had been influenced. AI systems now control far more information access than any single search engine ever did — making these stakes considerably higher in the present day.

↳ The search engine manipulation effect (SEME) — PNAS, 112(33), 2015
EU Artificial Intelligence Act · 2024

The EU AI Act — the world's first comprehensive AI law — classifies AI systems by risk level and bans certain applications outright, including real-time facial recognition in public spaces and social scoring systems. It establishes that some AI applications are fundamentally incompatible with basic human rights.

↳ Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 — The EU Artificial Intelligence Act
// Action Plan — Autonomy & Human Agency
STEP 01
Know when AI is deciding about you — you have the right to ask whether AI was used in decisions affecting you and to request human review of those decisions.
STEP 02
Diversify your information — if your news comes entirely from AI-curated feeds, you're experiencing a managed reality. Seek out direct, un-curated sources deliberately.
STEP 03
Engage with AI governance — regulations being written now will shape AI's role for decades. Participate in consultations, support advocacy organizations, and vote for tech-literate representatives.
STEP 04
Support AI transparency laws — advocate for requirements that AI used in consequential decisions must be explainable, auditable, and contestable.
STEP 05
Stay in the loop, not on autopilot — use AI as a tool you direct. The goal is augmented human capability, not replaced human judgment.
